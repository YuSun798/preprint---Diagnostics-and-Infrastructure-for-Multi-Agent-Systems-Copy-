9.4    Threats to Validity
Construct validity. The evidence-loss formalization (Eq. 1) assumes that the three conditions
(Ni , Di , Ri ) can be independently assessed. In practice, the need for evidence (Ni ) may depend
on the outcome of reconstruction attempts (Ri ), creating a circular dependency that the static
formalization does not capture.

External validity. The surveyed systems primarily target text-based LLM agents. Multi-
modal agents (combining vision, audio, or embodied action) may introduce state types not
covered by the current taxonomy. The DRICE framework’s dimensions are designed to be
extensible, but we have not validated them on non-text workloads.


10      Conclusion
This review has mapped 48 diagnostic methods and serving infrastructure systems, comple-
mented by 12 evaluation benchmarks, for foundation model-based multi-agent systems, orga-
nizing them by functional role and characterizing them along six method dimensions. Four
main findings emerge.
    First, the diagnostic-infrastructure interaction is conditional: evidence loss requires that
needed diagnostic state is discarded from a shared resource before the diagnostic horizon and
cannot be reconstructed (Eq. 1). Most current architectures avoid this scenario by storing traces
independently of serving state, placing the majority of systems in Tier A (independent opera-
tion). Among the T1 systems surveyed, none demonstrated a production case where KV-cache
eviction destroyed diagnostic evidence.
    Second, the DRICE evaluation framework reveals a systematic D–R gap: diagnostic meth-
ods consume rich evidence but do not interact with retention policies, while infrastructure sys-
tems implement sophisticated retention but are unaware of diagnostic value. The policy-driven
runtime and TOPAS partially bridge this gap — the former feeds observability signals into
eviction, the latter jointly optimizes prefix retention and scheduling under a shared capacity
constraint — but within our curated corpus, we did not identify a system that jointly optimizes
diagnostic retention and serving throughput.
    Third, the retention ablation on real TraceElephant traces (220 failures, 5 task suites from
3 MAS frameworks) reveals a subsampling paradox: heuristic attributors achieve higher accu-
racy at 10% retention than at full retention, because subsampling removes normal events that
cause false recovery signals. Importance-weighted sampling provides only marginal improve-
ment over uniform random at low budgets (+0.008, within noise), suggesting the benefit derives
from noise removal rather than from the specific importance scores. This finding characterizes
a heuristic–retention interaction and motivates retention-aware diagnostic design, though repli-
cation with LLM-judge attribution methods is necessary to establish generality.
    Fourth, the diagnostic landscape is rapidly consolidating around zero-replay, single-trajectory
methods: 10 of 12 failure-localization methods require no environment re-execution. Training-
free signal-extraction approaches (MASPrism, 0.6B model, 2.66s/case) achieve competitive

moves the field from isolated component optimization toward integrated diagnostic-aware sys-
