The evidence-aware comparison above supports a practical conclusion: diagnostic fidelity and serving efficiency cannot be jointly optimized without specifying exactly which state is preserved, which signals are available before a failure, and which costs accrue on the execution path. This section turns that observation into a falsifiable protocol rather than presenting prospective directions as measured results.

\subsection{Trade-offs and alternative explanations}
Statistical replay attribution may use fewer learned parameters but requires repeated environment execution, stable restart conditions, and potentially large tool-call budgets~\cite{famas2025}. LLM-based reasoning can avoid environment replay but consumes inference tokens and depends on trace completeness. RL-trained attribution moves part of the burden to offline training; its model parameter count alone does not reveal training expense. Prefill-only methods such as MASPrism~\cite{masprism2026} avoid task-specific training but rely on reliable signal extraction from a smaller model. No single approach is uniformly preferable; the choice depends on the availability of detailed traces, permitted replay, desired root-cause granularity, and latency target.

Serving-side mechanisms also have conditional value. Prefix caches are effective when token sequences and model states are compatible; application-level activation predictions help when workflow structure has predictive power, not merely when a workload is called multi-agent. A cache miss with preserved logs may only increase computation. An independently dropped tool observation may destroy failure evidence even when the KV cache is retained. These counterexamples weaken any deterministic formulation of the retention tension and sharpen the conditions in Eq.~\ref{eq:loss}.

\subsection{Instrumented evaluation protocol}\label{sec:validation}
We propose a \emph{not-yet-executed} two-stage protocol. Stage A uses labeled multi-agent traces with controllable evidence ablations to measure attribution sensitivity. Candidate data include TraceElephant~\cite{traceelephant2026} and replayable Who\&When cases~\cite{whenwhen2025}. Stage B uses a serving harness or an instrumented replay engine that records request/agent IDs, tool spans, causal edges, cache key hashes, model and adapter versions, memory-tier moves, telemetry dropped at capture, restoration attempts, and per-event timestamps. The harness must distinguish a dropped event from an event that was recorded but resides on a slow tier.

\paragraph*{Clock and intervention assumptions.} Decisions are made at time $t$ from information observable no later than $t$; failure labels and future tool outcomes are withheld. A diagnostic alarm is eligible for scoring only if raised before the failure manifestation time $t_f$ and before any prohibited action, not after reviewing the completed trajectory. A scheduler may change priority, protect a checkpoint, increase logging, or initiate a guardrail; each action is logged with its decision timestamp and overhead. Offline full-trace attribution is evaluated separately to avoid incorrectly crediting post-hoc knowledge to an online policy. Synchronous diagnosis competes with the user-facing latency SLO, while an asynchronous detector is charged its compute, storage, and eventual recovery delay rather than assumed to be free.

\paragraph*{Baselines and controlled factors.} Compare (B0) uniform trace retention plus standard LRU/TTL serving; (B1) equal-byte random or age-based evidence sampling; (B2) reuse-probability-only retention/prefetch; (B3) diagnostic-proxy-only retention; and (B4) a joint proxy-plus-reuse policy. Equalize storage bytes, active GPU capacity, task mix, model versions, and initial failure rate. Sweep trace sampling levels, cache capacities, diagnostic delay, snapshot availability, branch unpredictability, and workload burstiness. Measure each policy both under a matched replay corpus and randomized or paired failure-injection runs. Never use future failure labels as online inputs.

\paragraph*{Metrics.} Report agent- and step-level top-1/top-$k$ attribution accuracy and calibration; task completion/success, violated-policy rate and recovery success; GPU and durable-storage bytes, trace retention ratio, cache hit rate, prefill recomputation, network I/O, p50/p95/p99 latency, throughput, and power where instrumented. For early alarms, report false alarms per 1,000 agent-hours (or another explicit exposure denominator), precision/recall at an alert budget, detection lead time $\max(0,t_f-t_{\mathrm{alarm}})$ for true pre-failure alarms, and time to containment/recovery. Include confidence intervals, workload definitions, diagnostic model calls, and measured end-to-end compute/latency rather than mixing replay count, tokens and parameter count.

\paragraph*{Estimating diagnostic value without future labels.} An online importance proxy $\hat v_i(t)$ may combine contemporaneous signals: tool error/timeout and policy flags, disagreement among agents, unexpected state transitions, rarity of an observation, dependency-graph fan-out, failure-predictor uncertainty, and the estimated irrecoverability of external evidence. Predictors can be calibrated on a separate \emph{historical} training split and frozen before evaluation. A minimum audit log must be maintained for all conditions; hard privacy and retention constraints cannot be overridden by the proxy. Compare the proxy with simple age/size heuristics and an \emph{oracle using future labels only as a retrospective upper bound}. Report performance under proxy miscalibration and distribution shift.

\subsection{Source verification and limitations}
This is a curated critical review. The original collection was not a systematic database search, and the infrastructure literature in the source manuscript was predominantly examined at abstract level. Therefore, mechanism-level assumptions in Table~\ref{tab:infrastructure} are useful hypotheses, not verified characterizations of every implementation. A negative claim about the nonexistence of a joint diagnostic--serving budget cannot be established by these sources; full-text checks of influential scheduling, KV cache, and observability work are a prerequisite for such a claim. The repository's evidence-audit checklist records the outstanding verification work and source-location requirements. Experimental headline results from disparate systems cannot be treated as a common leaderboard.

\subsection{Privacy, security, and scope}
Richer retention may preserve credentials, personal data, tool outputs, and intermediate internal reasoning. Privacy-preserving observability requires access control, redaction, encryption, and bounded lifetimes even when longer retention might improve failure attribution~\cite{auditableagents2026,opentelemetry2026}. The analysis addresses diagnostics and selected serving functions only; it does not purport to review all orchestration, networking, data-governance, or model-training components of a production stack. This limited interface is the reason to state explicit compatibility and causal conditions rather than claim a complete architecture.
