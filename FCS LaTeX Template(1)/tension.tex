The reviewed diagnostic methods consume execution evidence; serving policies manage objects that may be expensive to keep available. The relationship is \emph{conditional}, not an identity between serving-cache eviction and diagnostic data loss. In particular, a KV cache is a computational acceleration structure, whereas a persisted trace is an execution record. Removing the former often induces only a new prefill pass. The latter may be irreplaceable if a tool response, stochastic environment observation, or causal ordering was never logged.

\paragraph*{State types and dependency boundaries.}
Table~\ref{tab:tension} distinguishes the physical state, its consumer, and the outcome of reclamation. Co-location alone does not create a diagnostic dependency: a trace in an independently retained object store survives GPU KV eviction. Conversely, diagnostic information can be lost even without cache eviction when telemetry sampling discards an unrepeatable tool observation. Model weights and adapter versions matter for reproducibility, but saving a model checkpoint is not equivalent to preserving the environment, random seeds, tool permissions, or external API responses. Replaying a sequence is reliable only if those dependencies are restored or their differences controlled.

\paragraph*{When is the conflict real?}
For object $i$ and diagnostic horizon $T$, define $N_i(T)$ as the event that a later diagnosis needs information uniquely carried by that object; $D_i(T)$ that the relevant information is discarded before the horizon; and $R_i(T)$ that its content can be reconstructed faithfully at acceptable cost. An \emph{evidence-loss} event requires all three conditions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
L_i(T)=N_i(T)\wedge D_i(T)\wedge\neg R_i(T).
\end{equation}
By contrast, the case $N_i\wedge D_i\wedge R_i$ incurs recovery or prefill cost but need not reduce attribution quality. A cross-layer \emph{resource} conflict additionally requires that diagnostic capture/retention and serving share a genuinely constrained memory, storage, bandwidth, or latency budget. This test excludes the misleading generalization that every KV eviction degrades observability. It also identifies two experimentally separable effects: loss of information versus delayed access to information.

\paragraph*{Evidence and counterexamples.}
TraceElephant~\cite{traceelephant2026} compares full and output-only traces under the same All-at-Once protocol: agent-level attribution increases from 51\% to 62.2\%, while step-level attribution increases from 16\% to 28.1\%. This supports the \emph{importance of trace completeness} but does not demonstrate a serving policy caused trace loss. ScaleSim~\cite{zhou2026} uses invocation-distance-aware movement of inactive state; moving that state off the fastest tier is not equivalent to destroying an independently stored diagnostic log. Continuum~\cite{continuum2025} uses cache lifetime policies; a TTL expiry can increase prefill work even if all messages remain recorded. These are examples of serving-state decisions, not verified demonstrations of cross-layer evidence deletion.

Replay-based localization such as FAMAS~\cite{famas2025} and intervention-based DoVer~\cite{dover2026} depends on access to a suitable execution environment. If checkpoints and external observations are not available, the diagnostic intervention may be invalid; if they are retained separately, cache reclamation may simply delay resumption. TrajAudit~\cite{trajaudit2026} compresses observation context before analysis, illustrating a \emph{within-diagnostics} fidelity--cost decision, not a measured serving conflict. The distinction between absent evidence, unavailable checkpoints, and a slower cache miss must therefore be preserved in interpretation and figures.

\paragraph*{Contribution and evidence limit.}
The contribution is the explicit decision boundary in Eq.~\ref{eq:loss}, the state-object matrix, and an evaluation agenda that can falsify a proposed shared-budget policy. The current literature sample, heavily dependent on abstract-level descriptions for infrastructure, does \emph{not} support a universal assertion that no existing system jointly manages diagnostic and serving state. It supports the narrower observation that a jointly measured diagnostic-fidelity/serving-cost frontier has not been demonstrated in the evidence examined here. Full-text, source-located verification of representative serving papers remains necessary before making stronger negative claims.
