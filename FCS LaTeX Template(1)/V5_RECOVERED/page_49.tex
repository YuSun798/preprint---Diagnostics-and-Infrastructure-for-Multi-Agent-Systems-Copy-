\noindent 9.4 Threats to Validity\par
\noindent Construct validity. The evidence-loss formalization (Eq. 1) assumes that the three conditions\par
\noindent (Ni , Di , Ri ) can be independently assessed. In practice, the need for evidence (Ni ) may depend\par
\noindent on the outcome of reconstruction attempts (Ri ), creating a circular dependency that the static\par
\noindent formalization does not capture.\par
\par\smallskip
\noindent External validity. The surveyed systems primarily target text-based LLM agents. Multi-\par
\noindent modal agents (combining vision, audio, or embodied action) may introduce state types not\par
\noindent covered by the current taxonomy. The DRICE framework’s dimensions are designed to be\par
\noindent extensible, but we have not validated them on non-text workloads.\par

\noindent 10 Conclusion\par
\noindent This review has mapped 48 diagnostic methods and serving infrastructure systems, comple-\par
\noindent mented by 12 evaluation benchmarks, for foundation model-based multi-agent systems, orga-\par
\noindent nizing them by functional role and characterizing them along six method dimensions. Four\par
\noindent main findings emerge.\par
\noindent  First, the diagnostic-infrastructure interaction is conditional: evidence loss requires that\par
\noindent needed diagnostic state is discarded from a shared resource before the diagnostic horizon and\par
\noindent cannot be reconstructed (Eq. 1). Most current architectures avoid this scenario by storing traces\par
\noindent independently of serving state, placing the majority of systems in Tier A (independent opera-\par
\noindent tion). Among the T1 systems surveyed, none demonstrated a production case where KV-cache\par
\noindent eviction destroyed diagnostic evidence.\par
\noindent  Second, the DRICE evaluation framework reveals a systematic D–R gap: diagnostic meth-\par
\noindent ods consume rich evidence but do not interact with retention policies, while infrastructure sys-\par
\noindent tems implement sophisticated retention but are unaware of diagnostic value. The policy-driven\par
\noindent runtime and TOPAS partially bridge this gap — the former feeds observability signals into\par
\noindent eviction, the latter jointly optimizes prefix retention and scheduling under a shared capacity\par
\noindent constraint — but within our curated corpus, we did not identify a system that jointly optimizes\par
\noindent diagnostic retention and serving throughput.\par
\noindent  Third, the retention ablation on real TraceElephant traces (220 failures, 5 task suites from\par
\noindent 3 MAS frameworks) reveals a subsampling paradox: heuristic attributors achieve higher accu-\par
\noindent racy at 10\% retention than at full retention, because subsampling removes normal events that\par
\noindent cause false recovery signals. Importance-weighted sampling provides only marginal improve-\par
\noindent ment over uniform random at low budgets (+0.008, within noise), suggesting the benefit derives\par
\noindent from noise removal rather than from the specific importance scores. This finding characterizes\par
\noindent a heuristic–retention interaction and motivates retention-aware diagnostic design, though repli-\par
\noindent cation with LLM-judge attribution methods is necessary to establish generality.\par
