\noindent Training-free signal extraction. MASPrism [19] extracts diagnostic signals directly from\par
\noindent a 0.6B-parameter model’s (Qwen3-0.6B) prefill stage without any training, replays, or output-\par
\noindent token generation. The method operates in two deterministic prefill passes (§3.2–3.3, confirmed).\par
\noindent In the first pass (Filtering), next-token NLL scores and attention patterns identify symptomatic\par
\noindent steps: tokens with unexpectedly high NLL indicate points where the execution deviates from\par
\noindent the model’s expectations. The top 20–50\% of steps by NLL score (configurable per bench-\par
\noindent mark) are retained as symptom candidates (§4.1.3). In the second pass (Diagnosis), the retained\par
\noindent candidates are restored into context and rescored; attention weight patterns link symptoms to\par
\noindent root-cause candidates through a ranked scoring mechanism.\par
\noindent  The method requires zero training, zero replays, and zero output tokens — only two deter-\par
\noindent ministic prefill passes. It processes trajectories at 2.66 seconds on average on an RTX 5090,\par
\noindent consuming only 7,066 input tokens versus 17,748 for A2P (§5, Table 4). This yields 6.69×\par
\noindent speed improvement over comparable approaches. On TRAIL/SWE-bench, MASPrism outper-\par
\noindent forms Gemini-2.5-Pro by 89.50\% relative (Table 1). Model robustness testing across different\par
\noindent backbone sizes (Table 5) confirms that the 0.6B model provides the best cost-accuracy trade-off.\par
